<section id="about" class="syllabus-section">
  <h2>About this course</h2>
  <p>What does it mean to see the world, and how can a computer see? CSE 185 introduces the foundations of computer vision &mdash; from low-level image processing to modern deep learning models that recognize, segment, and reconstruct the visual world.</p>
  <p>We will cover <strong>visual representations, projective geometry, image understanding, and object recognition</strong>, including topics like feature detection, image filtering, segmentation, motion analysis, 3D reconstruction (stereo, photometric stereo, structure from motion), and neural networks. Time permitting, we'll explore face recognition, pose estimation, detection &amp; tracking, action recognition, transformer networks, and vision&ndash;language models.</p>

  <h3>Prerequisites</h3>
  <p><strong>Required:</strong> CSE 031, CSE 100, MATH 024.<br>
  <strong>Strongly recommended:</strong> Linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability/statistics, Python, and data structures &amp; algorithms.</p>
</section>

<section id="assignments" class="syllabus-section">
  <h2>Assignments</h2>

  <p>There are <strong>four assignments</strong>, one per course unit. Each combines the conceptual practice and the hands-on coding work for that unit into a single deliverable.</p>

  <h3>Working on them</h3>
  <p>Lab sessions are your built-in work time, with TAs on hand to help. Attendance isn't graded, but the labs are where most students do their best learning. If your section fills up or you want extra time, you're welcome to drop into additional sections.</p>
  <p><strong>You're encouraged to discuss concepts with classmates</strong>, but every solution you turn in must be written by you, in your own words.</p>

  <h3>Submitting your work</h3>
  <p>Assignments are submitted on Gradescope. Mark all pages associated with each problem &mdash; unmarked pages receive a 0 for that problem and will not be regraded.</p>

  <h3>Late work &amp; slip days</h3>
  <p>Everyone gets <strong>4 slip days</strong> to use across the four assignments. One slip day extends a deadline by 24 hours. They're meant for illness, submission issues, and other unforeseen circumstances &mdash; use them wisely and save them for when you actually need them. No need to ask &mdash; just submit late and we'll count it.</p>
  <p>Once you're out of slip days, late work earns no credit &mdash; no exceptions. Staff actively prioritizes support for assignments whose deadlines haven't passed, so plan ahead. All work must be submitted by <strong>11:59 PM on the final exam date</strong>; we won't accept submissions after that point, including slip days, so grades can go out on time.</p>
  <p>Hitting a real obstacle (illness, family emergency, technical disaster)? Reach out <em>early</em> &mdash; the sooner we know, the more we can do.</p>
</section>

  <h2>Policies</h2>

  <details class="policy">
    <summary><strong>Academic integrity</strong></summary>
    <p>Academic honesty is taken very seriously at UC Merced. <strong>Talk to each other about concepts &mdash; that's how you learn.</strong> But when it's time to turn in a solution, write it yourself.</p>
    <p>Not allowed:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Sharing or receiving solutions before or after a deadline.</li>
      <li>Submitting a friend's work to test the autograder.</li>
      <li>Examining someone else's solution for "ideas."</li>
      <li>Submitting ChatGPT / GitHub Copilot output as your own solution.</li>
      <li>Posting course solutions or materials publicly.</li>
      <li>Code obfuscation to dodge detection.</li>
    </ul>
    <p><strong>Penalties:</strong> first offense &rarr; the negative of the assignment's maximum points (and it cannot be dropped). Second offense &rarr; automatic F in the course. All cases are reported to the Office of Student Rights and Responsibilities.</p>
